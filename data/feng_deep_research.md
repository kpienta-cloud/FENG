# Felix Y. Feng, MD (1975–2024): Scientific Career and Legacy
### Source-cited research report prepared for a keynote at the Feng Symposium on Prostate Cancer Revolutions

**Scope note.** Every factual claim below is followed by a markdown link to the exact page the claim was taken from. Items that could not be confirmed from a fetched source are marked **n.a.** Two important discrepancies with the framing in the research brief are flagged in Section 7 and in "Open Questions / Caveats" at the end.

## 0. Biographical anchor facts

- Feng was born in 1975 in Palo Alto, California; he attended Stanford as an undergraduate (where he met his wife, Mary), earned his MD at Washington University in St. Louis, and trained in residency at the University of Michigan ([Wikipedia: Felix Feng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Feng)).
- A first-person profile adds that he was born in Stamford, Connecticut, spent about three years in Taiwan as a child before the family settled in Palo Alto, that all four of his grandparents died of cancer, and that he began in radiology residency before switching to radiation oncology; he names Ted Lawrence, Arul Chinnaiyan, Howard Sandler, and Lori Pierce as mentors ([The ASCO Post, "Felix Feng Strives for Balance Between Work and Family Life," Jan 25, 2020](https://ascopost.com/issues/january-25-2020/felix-feng-strives-for-balance-between-work-and-family-life/)).
- He did his postdoctoral training in Arul Chinnaiyan's laboratory, spent six years on the University of Michigan faculty, and joined UCSF in 2016, rising to professor of radiation oncology, urology and medicine ([Nature Cancer obituary, "Felix Feng (1975–2024)"](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43018-025-00961-y)).
- At Michigan he directed the division of translational genomics, co-led the multidisciplinary prostate clinic, and directed the genitourinary program in radiation oncology ([UCSF News, Jan 2025](https://www.ucsf.edu/news/2025/01/429356/prostate-cancer-expert-felix-feng-dies-at-48)).
- At UCSF he was professor of radiation oncology, urology and medicine; vice chair of translational research at the UCSF Helen Diller Family Comprehensive Cancer Center; and co-director of the UCSF Prostate Cancer Program with Eric J. Small ([Cancer Letter republication of the UCSF obituary](https://cancerletter.com/obituary/20250124_4/)).
- He died of cancer on December 10, 2024, at age 48 ([Cancer Letter / UCSF obituary](https://cancerletter.com/obituary/20250124_4/)).
- Productivity: 434 publications, averaging 23 per year — "nearly two per month, every month of every year"; he was ranked in the top 1% of U.S. scientists by an internationally recognized productivity/impact metric ([Cancer Letter / UCSF obituary](https://cancerletter.com/obituary/20250124_4/)). A separate Prostate Cancer Foundation legacy lecture states more than 500 publications before age 50, roughly 30 papers appearing posthumously in 2025, and approximately 3,200 distinct collaborators/co-authors, about half of them international ([Prostate Cancer Foundation, "The Ongoing Legacy of Felix Feng: Team Science"](https://www.pcf.org/the-ongoing-legacy-of-felix-feng-team-science/)).
- Eric J. Small: "Felix Feng was an exceptional scientist—a once-in-a-generation scientist—and his staggering intellect and scientific insights led to his remarkable scientific accomplishments... But as important as his scientific successes were, Felix could build communities of individuals, collaborations and teams like no one else" ([Cancer Letter / UCSF obituary](https://cancerletter.com/obituary/20250124_4/)).
- A formal memorial appeared in European Urology: Beltran H., *European Urology* 2025;87(3):376–377, doi 10.1016/j.eururo.2024.12.008, PMID 39977953 ([PubMed 39977953](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39977953/)).

## 1. Discovery science era (Michigan, ~2007–2016)

### 1.1 Long non-coding RNAs: PCAT-1 and SChLAP1

- **PCAT-1 discovery.** Prensner JR et al., "Transcriptome sequencing across a prostate cancer cohort identifies PCAT-1, an unannotated lincRNA implicated in disease progression," *Nature Biotechnology* 2011;29:742–749, doi 10.1038/nbt.1914. RNA-seq across 102 prostate tissues and cell lines identified 121 unannotated prostate cancer–associated ncRNA transcripts (PCATs), of which PCAT-1 was a prostate-specific regulator of cell proliferation implicated in disease progression ([Nature Biotechnology](https://www.nature.com/articles/nbt.1914)). *Feng's authorship on this specific paper was not confirmed from the fetched page: **n.a.** — but his own laboratory site lists the PCAT-1 program among his work (see below).*
- **PCAT-1 mechanism (BRCAness).** Prensner JR, ... Chinnaiyan AM, **Feng FY** (final author), "PCAT-1, a long noncoding RNA, regulates BRCA2 and controls homologous recombination in cancer," *Cancer Research* 2014;74(6):1651–1660, doi 10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-13-3159, PMID 24473064. This was described as the first role for a long non-coding RNA in double-stranded DNA break repair in prostate cancer: PCAT-1 post-transcriptionally represses the BRCA2 3′ UTR, producing a functional homologous-recombination deficiency and conferring high sensitivity to small-molecule PARP1 inhibitors — "a novel mechanism of 'BRCAness' in sporadic cancers" ([PubMed 24473064](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24473064/)).
- **PCAT-1 and c-MYC.** A companion 2014 *Neoplasia* paper showed PCAT-1 promotes prostate cell proliferation via post-transcriptional up-regulation of cMyc through the *MYC* 3′ UTR ([Neoplasia 2014, PMC4240923](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4240923/)). Feng's own lab page lists "PCAT1 up-regulates c-MYC (Prensner, Neoplasia 2014)" among his group's findings ([Feng Laboratory, Ongoing Projects](http://fenglaboratory.com/ongoing-projects)).
- **SChLAP1 discovery and mechanism.** Prensner JR et al., "The long noncoding RNA SChLAP1 promotes aggressive prostate cancer and antagonizes the SWI/SNF complex," *Nature Genetics* 2013;45:1392–1398, doi 10.1038/ng.2771. SChLAP1 (also annotated LINC00913) is outlier-overexpressed in ~25% of prostate cancers, is required for cancer cell invasiveness and metastasis, and acts by antagonizing the tumor-suppressive SWI/SNF chromatin-remodeling complex; F.Y.F. is credited in the author-contribution statement for clinical analyses and the Mayo cohort, with DoD support PC094231 ([Nature Genetics](https://www.nature.com/articles/ng.2771)).
- **SChLAP1 as a clinical-grade biomarker.** Prensner JR et al., **Feng FY senior/last author**, "RNA biomarkers associated with metastatic progression in prostate cancer: a multi-institutional high-throughput analysis of SChLAP1," *Lancet Oncology* 2014;15(13):1469–1480, doi 10.1016/S1470-2045(14)71113-1, PMID 25456366. In 1,008 prostatectomy patients (545 discovery, 463 validation), high SChLAP1 expression independently predicted 10-year metastasis (OR 2.45, 95% CI 1.70–3.53, p<0.0001) ([PubMed 25456366](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25456366/)).
- Feng's laboratory also published a broader survey of the non-coding landscape of prostate cancer (Prensner et al., *Lancet Oncology* 2015), per the lab's own project description ([Feng Laboratory](http://fenglaboratory.com/ongoing-projects)).
- A further lncRNA paper on AR-regulated ARLNC1 appeared as Zhang et al., *Nature Genetics* 2018;50(6):814–824, doi 10.1038/s41588-018-0120-1 ([Nature Genetics](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-018-0120-1)). *Feng's authorship on that paper was not verified from the fetched page: **n.a.***

### 1.2 TMPRSS2–ERG, PARP1, and DNA-PK: the "ETS fusion → DNA repair" axis

- **Landmark paper.** Brenner JC et al. (**Feng FY** second-to-last author), "Mechanistic rationale for inhibition of poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase in ETS gene fusion-positive prostate cancer," *Cancer Cell* 2011;19(5):664–678, doi 10.1016/j.ccr.2011.04.010, PMID 21575865. The work showed that the TMPRSS2:ERG fusion product physically interacts with PARP1 and DNA-PKcs, that ERG-mediated transcription requires PARP1 and DNA-PKcs, and that PARP1 inhibition blocks growth of ETS-positive prostate cancer xenografts — providing the mechanistic rationale for testing PARP inhibitors in ETS-fusion-positive prostate cancer ([PubMed 21575865](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21575865/)).
- **PARP1 as an AR co-activator.** Feng's lab page cites Schiewer et al., *Cancer Discovery* 2012, for PARP1 acting as a co-activator of androgen receptor signaling ([Feng Laboratory](http://fenglaboratory.com/ongoing-projects)).
- **The hormone–DNA repair circuit.** Goodwin JF et al., "A hormone-DNA repair circuit governs the response to genotoxic insult," *Cancer Discovery* 2013;3(11):1254–1271, doi 10.1158/2159-8290.CD-13-0108, established a feedback loop between AR signaling and DNA-PK–mediated repair ([PubMed 24027197](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24027197/)). Feng's lab page describes this as the discovery that "DNAPK and AR are involved in a feedback loop (Goodwin, Cancer Discovery 2013)" ([Feng Laboratory](http://fenglaboratory.com/ongoing-projects)).
- **DNA-PKcs as a transcriptional driver of metastasis.** Goodwin JF et al. (**Feng FY** 28th of 29 authors; Karen Knudsen last author), "DNA-PKcs-Mediated Transcriptional Regulation Drives Prostate Cancer Progression and Metastasis," *Cancer Cell* 2015;28(1):97–113, doi 10.1016/j.ccell.2015.06.004, PMID 26175416 ([PubMed 26175416](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26175416/)). *In vitro* and *in vivo* interrogation demonstrated DNA-PKcs functions as a selective transcriptional regulator driving progression and metastasis ([PMC4531387](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4531387/)).
- **Why this mattered translationally.** NRG Oncology's tribute states that Feng's preclinical work on PARP1, DNA-PK and bromodomain biology "seeded" subsequent clinical trials of PARP1, DNA-PK and bromodomain inhibitors ([NRG Oncology legacy tribute](https://www.nrgoncology.org/Home/News/Post/legacy-tribute-in-honor-of-felix-feng-md/)). His lab page states his group's DNA-PK work led to a first-in-patient trial of a DNA-PK inhibitor combined with AR-directed therapy, and that the finding that BRD4 recruits AR to chromatin led to one of the first bromodomain-inhibitor trials in prostate cancer ([Feng Laboratory](http://fenglaboratory.com/ongoing-projects)).

### 1.3 Other molecular contributions from the Michigan/early UCSF period

- The Feng lab's project page also credits an immune-landscape analysis of prostate cancer (Zhao et al., *JNCI* 2012) and early biomarker-driven trial work (Hussain et al., *JCO* 2006) among the group's contributions ([Feng Laboratory](http://fenglaboratory.com/ongoing-projects)).
- **Metastatic prostate cancer genome architecture.** Quigley DA et al., "Genomic Hallmarks and Structural Variation in Metastatic Prostate Cancer," *Cell* 2018;174(3):758–769.e9, PMID 30033370 ([PubMed 30033370](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30033370/)); this is cited as core work on his lab's own site ([Feng Laboratory](http://fenglaboratory.com/ongoing-projects)).
- **Epigenomic and 3D-genome work at UCSF.** His group produced the 5-hydroxymethylcytosine landscape of prostate cancer (*Cancer Research* 2022;82(21):3888–3902), an integrated analysis of the three-dimensional genome with DNA, RNA and epigenomic alterations in metastatic prostate cancer (*Nature Genetics* 2024;56(8):1689–1700, PMID 39020220, Feng last author), and an alternative-promoter-usage analysis from ultra-deep RNA-seq (*Nature Cell Biology* 2024;26(7):1176–1186, PMID 38871824) ([UCSF Benioff Initiative for Prostate Cancer Research, featured publications](https://cancer.ucsf.edu/research/benioff-prostate-initiative)).
- Jonathan Chou describes this strand of work as "pioneering approaches to explore the genomic, epigenomic and 3D chromatin landscapes from tiny biopsy samples of metastatic prostate cancer" ([Cancer Letter / UCSF obituary](https://cancerletter.com/obituary/20250124_4/)).
- **AR inhibition and immune visibility.** Chesner LN et al. (Feng among senior authors), "Androgen Receptor Inhibition Increases MHC Class I Expression and Improves Immune Response in Prostate Cancer," *Cancer Discovery* 2025;15(3):481–494, PMID 39652470 ([UCSF BIPCR publications](https://cancer.ucsf.edu/research/benioff-prostate-initiative)).

## 2. Genomic classifiers and precision medicine

### 2.1 What Decipher is

- The Decipher Prostate Genomic Classifier is a 22-gene, whole-transcriptome-derived signature developed with machine learning; it is run on biopsy or prostatectomy tissue and returns an absolute risk of developing metastasis with standard treatment, so clinicians can de-escalate for low-risk patients or escalate/act earlier for high-risk patients ([Veracyte press release, Dec 5, 2024](https://investor.veracyte.com/news-releases/news-release-details/veracytes-decipher-prostate-test-now-only-gene-expression-test)).

### 2.2 Feng's signature classifier studies

- **PORTOS — predicting benefit *from radiation itself*.** Zhao SG et al. (**Feng FY** last author), "Development and validation of a 24-gene predictor of response to postoperative radiotherapy in prostate cancer: a matched, retrospective analysis," *Lancet Oncology* 2016;17(11):1612–1620, doi 10.1016/S1470-2045(16)30491-0, PMID 27743920. Training cohort n=196, validation n=330. Among patients with a high PORTOS score, postoperative radiotherapy was associated with 10-year metastasis of 5% vs 63% without radiotherapy (HR 0.12); in validation, 4% vs 35% (HR 0.15). Critically, in patients with low PORTOS scores radiotherapy conferred no benefit, and the Decipher, CAPRA-S and CCP scores did **not** predict radiotherapy response — establishing PORTOS as *predictive* rather than merely prognostic ([PubMed 27743920](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27743920/)).
- **Luminal/basal subtyping and hormone-therapy benefit.** Zhao SG et al. (**Feng FY** last of 28 authors), "Associations of Luminal and Basal Subtyping of Prostate Cancer With Prognosis and Response to Androgen Deprivation Therapy," *JAMA Oncology* 2017;3(12):1663–1672, doi 10.1001/jamaoncol.2017.0751, PMID 28494073. Across 3,782 samples, luminal B tumors had the worst prognosis, and only luminal B patients derived benefit from postoperative androgen deprivation (10-year metastasis 33% with ADT vs 55% without; p for interaction = .006) ([PubMed 28494073](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28494073/)).
- **NRG/RTOG 9601 ancillary study — the practice-changing one.** **Feng FY (first author)**, Huang HC, Spratt DE et al., "Validation of a 22-Gene Genomic Classifier in Patients With Recurrent Prostate Cancer: An Ancillary Study of the NRG/RTOG 9601 Randomized Clinical Trial," *JAMA Oncology* 2021;7(4):544–552, doi 10.1001/jamaoncol.2020.7671 (trial NCT00002874). 352 of 760 randomized patients were analyzable. Per 0.1 unit of genomic classifier score, distant metastasis HR 1.17 (p=.006), prostate-cancer-specific mortality HR 1.39 (p<.001), overall survival HR 1.17 (p=.002). The 12-year absolute overall-survival benefit from adding bicalutamide to salvage radiation was 2.4% in low-GC patients versus 8.9% in high-GC patients; restricted to men receiving early salvage radiation (PSA <0.7 ng/mL), the effect was −7.8% (low GC) versus +4.6% (high GC) — i.e., low-GC men appeared to be harmed rather than helped ([JAMA Oncology full text](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaoncology/fullarticle/2776225); [Veracyte publication summary](https://www.veracyte.com/resources/scientific-publications/decipher-prostate-2021-04-jama-oncol-feng/)).
- **Multimodal deep learning on phase III trials.** Esteva A, Feng J, et al., "Prostate cancer therapy personalization via multi-modal deep learning on randomized phase III clinical trials," *npj Digital Medicine* 2022;5(1):71, doi 10.1038/s41746-022-00613-w, PMID 35676445 ([PubMed 35676445](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35676445/)). In his own words, this line of work used data from five phase III trials in localized prostate cancer with 10–20 years of follow-up to build a classifier that predicted 5- and 10-year metastasis significantly better than PSA and Gleason score, and was "the first study validated in samples from five phase III clinical trials" ([UroToday interview, Nov 2, 2022](http://www.urotoday.review/video-lectures/prostate-cancer/video/2914-personalizing-prostate-cancer-care-with-ai-felix-feng.html)).

### 2.3 How it changed practice

- UCSF states that his laboratory produced "the first clinical-grade biomarker panels that predict prostate cancer response to radiation or hormone therapy after surgery," and developed a DNA biomarker predicting resistance to targeted therapy ([Cancer Letter / UCSF obituary](https://cancerletter.com/obituary/20250124_4/)).
- Following the RTOG 9601 validation, the NCCN guidelines recommended the Decipher genomic test to guide post-prostatectomy treatment in high-risk patients ([Urology Times](https://www.urologytimes.com/view/nccn-recommends-decipher-genomic-test-to-guide-post-rp-treatment-in-high-risk-patients)).
- As of version 1 of the 2025 NCCN Clinical Practice Guidelines in Oncology for prostate cancer, Decipher Prostate is the **only** gene-expression test included in the "Advanced Tools" table of the Principles of Risk Stratification and Biomarkers section (PROS-H); Veracyte states the test has been validated in dozens of studies involving more than 100,000 patients and is the most widely used molecular test in prostate cancer ([Veracyte press release, Dec 5, 2024](https://investor.veracyte.com/news-releases/news-release-details/veracytes-decipher-prostate-test-now-only-gene-expression-test)).
- A Prostate Cancer Foundation legacy lecture states Decipher has now been validated in 14 phase III randomized trials and used in more than 250,000 people ([Prostate Cancer Foundation](https://www.pcf.org/the-ongoing-legacy-of-felix-feng-team-science/)). *This figure comes from a lecture summary rather than a primary validation source; treat as attributed rather than independently verified.*

### 3.2 Circulating tumor DNA / cell-free DNA and resistance monitoring

- Quigley DA et al. (**Feng FY** last author), "Analysis of circulating cell-free DNA identifies multi-clonal heterogeneity of BRCA2 reversion mutations associated with resistance to PARP inhibitors," *Cancer Discovery* 2017;7(9):999–1005, doi 10.1158/2159-8290.CD-17-0146, PMID 28450426. Plasma cfDNA revealed seven distinct BRCA2 reversion alleles where a single tissue biopsy detected only two — establishing cfDNA as a route to detect polyclonal PARP-inhibitor resistance ([PMC5581695](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5581695/)).
- NRG Oncology's tribute credits Feng's team with developing "a plasma cell-free DNA biomarker predicting resistance to PARP1 inhibitors" ([NRG Oncology legacy tribute](https://www.nrgoncology.org/Home/News/Post/legacy-tribute-in-honor-of-felix-feng-md/)).
- Later cfDNA fragmentomics work: Helzer/Zhao et al., *Annals of Oncology* 2023;34(9):813–825, with Feng as a co-author.

### 3.3 Other therapeutic trials

- **BET/bromodomain inhibition.** Aggarwal RR et al., with **Felix Y Feng** as a listed author, "A Phase Ib/IIa Study of the Pan-BET Inhibitor ZEN-3694 in Combination with Enzalutamide in Patients with Metastatic Castration-resistant Prostate Cancer," *Clinical Cancer Research* 2020;26(20):5338–5347, doi 10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-20-1707, PMID 32694156. 75 patients with mCRPC resistant to abiraterone and/or enzalutamide; no MTD reached from 36–144 mg daily; grade ≥3 toxicity in 18.7%; median radiographic PFS 9.0 months (95% CI 4.6–12.9); **lower baseline AR transcriptional activity was associated with longer rPFS (10.4 vs 4.3 months)** — a biomarker-defined subgroup hypothesis ([PubMed 32694156](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32694156/)).
- **Biomarker-driven metastatic trials.** UCSF states Feng "was co-principal investigator on one of the first biomarker-driven clinical trials for patients with metastatic prostate cancer" ([Cancer Letter / UCSF obituary](https://cancerletter.com/obituary/20250124_4/)). The specific trial is not named in that source: **n.a.**
- **DLL3-directed immunotherapy and PET imaging** in small-cell neuroendocrine prostate cancer: Chou J et al. (Feng co-author), *Cancer Research* 2023;83(2):301–315, PMID 36351060 ([UCSF BIPCR publications](https://cancer.ucsf.edu/research/benioff-prostate-initiative)).
- **PARP inhibitor trials named in the brief (PROfound, TALAPRO).** No fetched source in this session documents Feng as an investigator on PROfound or TALAPRO: **n.a.** His lab page does cite Mateo et al., *NEJM* 2015 (the TOPARP olaparib study) as foundational context for the group's PARP work ([Feng Laboratory](http://fenglaboratory.com/ongoing-projects)).
- **CAN-2409 / aglatimagene besadenovec.** The phase 3 trial (PrTK03, NCT01436968) was reported by DeWeese et al., *Lancet Oncology* 2026;27(6):673–685, doi 10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00071-9, PMID 42225101: 745 men with localized prostate cancer, disease-free survival HR 0.70 (95% CI 0.52–0.94), p=0.016 ([PubMed 42225101](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42225101/)). **Feng is not an author on that publication**, and no fetched source — including the UCSF-authored obituary republished by the Cancer Letter — links Feng to CAN-2409. A related phase 2 trial during active surveillance (ULYSSES / PrTK04) is NCT02768363 ([ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02768363)). **Feng's role in CAN-2409: n.a. — the brief's premise could not be substantiated.**
- Feng also personally treated patients with high-risk and oligometastatic prostate cancer using intensity-modulated radiation therapy and stereotactic ablative radiotherapy ([Cancer Letter / UCSF obituary](https://cancerletter.com/obituary/20250124_4/)).

## 4. Companies founded

Both obituary sources state simply that "as an entrepreneur, he founded two companies aimed at individualizing cancer treatments for each patient" ([Cancer Letter / UCSF obituary](https://cancerletter.com/obituary/20250124_4/); also [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Feng)). Both are identified by name in his own ASTRO conflict-of-interest disclosures.

### 4.1 Artera (ArteraAI) — active

- Feng's ASTRO 2022 disclosure reads: "I am the co-founder of Artera (ARTificial intelligence for thERApeutics) and own stock," with the stated goal "to develop prognostic classifiers, in the context of prostate cancer, and possibly breast cancer" ([ASTRO 2022 combined COI disclosures PDF](https://www.astro.org/ASTRO/media/ASTRO/Meetings%20and%20Education/PDFs/AM22/AM22_CombinedCOI.pdf)).
- Artera's own site maintains a page for Feng as co-founder ([Artera: Felix Feng](https://artera.ai/team/felix-feng)); the company markets a multimodal AI (MMAI) test for prostate cancer and describes it as the first and only AI test included in the NCCN prostate cancer guidelines ([Artera](https://artera.ai/)).
- Co-founder Andre Esteva's profile states the two were introduced by Marc Benioff ([Modern Healthcare, 40 Under 40, 2025](https://www.modernhealthcare.com/awards/andre-esteva-40-under-40-2025)).
- Commercial trajectory: ArteraAI received a Medicare payment rate for the ArteraAI Prostate Cancer Test ([Artera news](https://artera.ai/news/arteraai-receives-medicare-payment-rate-for-the-arteraai-prostate-cancer-test)), and Tempus partnered with Artera to expand access ([Inside Precision Medicine](https://www.insideprecisionmedicine.com/topics/oncology/tempus-partners-with-artera-to-expand-personalized-prostate-cancer-treatment/)). **Status: active.**

## 5. Feng's own "big questions" and future vision

This is the most directly usable material for a keynote. Grouped by theme, with his own words where available.

### 5.1 Overtreatment, undertreatment, and "the right treatment to the right patient at the right time"

- "I think we're just scratching the surface of how we can personalize therapy." He framed his long-term goal as "getting the right treatment to the right patient at the right time," noting that across prostate cancer treatment scenarios some patients are overtreated, some undertreated, and some receive the right treatment — and that his aim was to expand the third group ([UroToday, "Personalizing Prostate Cancer Care with AI," Nov 2, 2022](http://www.urotoday.review/video-lectures/prostate-cancer/video/2914-personalizing-prostate-cancer-care-with-ai-felix-feng.html)).

### 5.2 Prostate cancer is not one disease

- "Not all prostate cancers are the same, but we have traditionally put prostate cancer into one disease... [it] could be genomically selected or stratified." Asked whether the future is smaller and more precise categories, he agreed ([Grand Rounds in Urology / Prostatepedia, "Frontiers in Prostate Cancer Genomics"](https://grandroundsinurology.com/frontiers-in-prostate-cancer-genomics/)).
- In the same interview he laid out the actionable genomic axes he saw as ready for the clinic: mismatch-repair deficiency directing immunotherapy, BRCA1/2 alterations directing PARP inhibitor trials, PTEN loss directing AKT inhibition, and AR splice variants as resistance mediators — and described NRG trials that embed Decipher and PAM50 prospectively ([Grand Rounds in Urology](https://grandroundsinurology.com/frontiers-in-prostate-cancer-genomics/)).
- He predicted that the era of using a **single** genomic biomarker across all clinical contexts would fade, and that each context — intermediate-risk, high-risk, metastatic — should have its own classifier personalized to those patients ([UroToday](http://www.urotoday.review/video-lectures/prostate-cancer/video/2914-personalizing-prostate-cancer-care-with-ai-felix-feng.html)).
- The Benioff Initiative he founded frames the same thesis institutionally: prostate cancer is "a heterogeneous disease," and the goal is to move "from a 'one-size-fits-all' approach, to a strategy where therapies are informed by the molecular mechanisms that govern a patient's individual cancer" ([UCSF Benioff Initiative for Prostate Cancer Research](https://cancer.ucsf.edu/research/benioff-prostate-initiative)).

### 5.3 Treatment resistance as the central problem

- "Being able to understand what drives treatment resistance and how to overcome treatment resistance... may very well be the key to curing cancers." And: "We're in the era of novel technologies... the era of artificial intelligence" ([V Foundation, "How Dr. Felix Feng Is Aiming for the Holy Grail in Prostate Cancer Research"](https://www.v.org/story/how-dr-felix-feng-is-aiming-for-the-holy-grail-in-prostate-cancer-research/)).
- "Thirty years from now, we can either cure all cancers or find a way to treat all cancers so that patients live with cancer, but... don't die of their cancer... I do recognize that's the holy grail, but why are you in science if you don't aim for the Holy Grail?" ([V Foundation](https://www.v.org/story/how-dr-felix-feng-is-aiming-for-the-holy-grail-in-prostate-cancer-research/)).
- His UCSF faculty profile states the aim of his research program as individualizing therapy by identifying the determinants of treatment resistance ([UCSF Radiation Oncology profile](https://radonc.ucsf.edu/about/our-team/felix-feng-md/)).
- The Benioff Initiative's stated rationale for why single-target therapy fails: "Cancer constantly evolves in response to medical therapies, the patient's immune system, their diet, and the environment of the organs to which the cancer metastasizes. This is why single-targeted therapies may not eradicate prostate cancer" ([UCSF BIPCR](https://cancer.ucsf.edu/research/benioff-prostate-initiative)). *This is an ecological/evolutionary framing of resistance and is directly relevant to a keynote on transformative science.*

### 5.4 AI, and the primacy of randomized phase III data

- "Artificial intelligence as a technology to help personalize decisions regarding patients, it's here. It's already here" — because "there's so much information that's been gathered about prostate cancer and other diseases over the last few decades," and "fundamentally, I think the principle of AI is to try to leverage all that information to help make better decisions" ([UroToday](http://www.urotoday.review/video-lectures/prostate-cancer/video/2914-personalizing-prostate-cancer-care-with-ai-felix-feng.html)).
- "The most valuable data sets out there are phase III clinical trials" — because randomization makes the results robust and believable; and because pathology slides prepared decades ago are prepared much like today's, archival slides can be digitized and reanalyzed with AI to build classifiers that remain valid in current practice ([UroToday](http://www.urotoday.review/video-lectures/prostate-cancer/video/2914-personalizing-prostate-cancer-care-with-ai-felix-feng.html)).
- He explicitly stated he was less interested in tools that diagnose cancer and more interested in tools that tell a patient who already has cancer "what treatments they should or shouldn't get" ([UroToday](http://www.urotoday.review/video-lectures/prostate-cancer/video/2914-personalizing-prostate-cancer-care-with-ai-felix-feng.html)).

### 5.5 Health equity as a technical requirement of AI, not an add-on

- He named the weakness of AI directly: it "works primarily in the patient populations from which the tool was built." He noted that despite many genomic tools in prostate cancer, the number of African American patients who have undergone genomic testing is "really, really low" relative to their share of the U.S. population. His team therefore built classifiers using cohorts approximately 20% African American and validated in similar populations; the resulting AI classifier performed equally well in Caucasian and African American patients. His conclusion: "the more inclusive the data set used to build the classifier, the better it will perform," and the tool "should be built to reflect the heterogeneity of people who develop prostate cancer" ([UroToday](http://www.urotoday.review/video-lectures/prostate-cancer/video/2914-personalizing-prostate-cancer-care-with-ai-felix-feng.html)).

### 5.6 Mentorship as the actual deliverable

- "I think that the magnitude of what we accomplish is not measured in just what we have done but measured in terms of the kind of collective efforts of everyone that you've helped along the way" ([Cancer Letter / UCSF obituary](https://cancerletter.com/obituary/20250124_4/)).
- "My greatest contribution to the field will be in helping develop the careers of the scientists and clinicians..." ([The ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/issues/january-25-2020/felix-feng-strives-for-balance-between-work-and-family-life/)).
- On the SPORE, articulating infrastructure as a scientific goal: "The infrastructure for our SPORE grant is designed to encourage and promote translation of innovative, impactful ideas and provide access to critical resources for bench-to-bedside transitions. We also aim to sponsor interdisciplinary collaborations and provide an effective framework for mentoring and nurturing the next generation of translational scientists" ([UCSF Helen Diller Family Comprehensive Cancer Center, Sept 3, 2024](https://cancer.ucsf.edu/news/2024/09/03/ucsf-prostate-cancer-program-awarded-spore-grant-by-national-cancer-institute)).

### 5.7 The inaugural symposium keynote — his last public "big questions" talk

- Giving the keynote at the first Feng Symposium on May 18, 2024, he opened: "When a symposium is named after you, you can pretty much just wax poetic up on stage... I always tell you to give a crisp talk—one or two major take-home messages. I'm gonna break all those rules, okay?" ([The Cancer Letter, "In the Archives"](https://cancerletter.com/in-the-archives/20250124_8/)).
